Experiment E — Exodus asymmetric-thrust capacitors vs Hively-style Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) / Scalar Wave (SW) detectors

A short educational sketch of a proposed side-by-side test for Exodus Propulsion Technologies (Titusville, FL; ept.space) and Dr. Lee Hively: whether asymmetric-thrust capacitors emit detectable Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) / Scalar Wave (SW) signatures beside Dan’s planned one Pelican-case equivalent (both the 1.3 GHz Experiment A and 433 MHz Experiment B detectors). Detectors are not built or verified yet. Warm skepticism welcome — published work by others (Hively, Monstein–Wesley, plasma-tube lineage) is encouragement, not Dan’s result.

1. Hypothesis

Do Exodus asymmetric electrostatic “thrust capacitors” radiate detectable Scalar Longitudinal Waves (SLW) and/or Scalar Waves (SW) in the sense of Hively’s Maxwell–Cartesian Electrodynamics (MCE) / Extended Electrodynamics (EED) framing, or leave a static / slowly varying vector-potential signature — while classical Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) fields are rejected by TEM-null antenna designs?

The capacitor may sit in a vacuum chamber with a Faraday cage around it, hypothetically transmitting (or not) in both static and modulated modes near 1.3 GHz and 433 MHz. Exodus public materials emphasize electrostatic pressure / third-order Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) narratives for thrust; this Experiment E asks a different, adjacent question: is there a radio-frequency (RF) / potential channel a Hively-style observer might notice?

Skeptical baseline. Null results are scientifically useful. A positive TEM-null excess would still need brutal controls before anyone whispers “longitudinal.” Dan has not yet built Experiment A (≈1.3 GHz bifilar/Faraday) or Experiment B (433 MHz sphere) detectors.

2. Two capacitor modes

Static null mode

Capacitor charged; power supply removed; Exodus-style claims sometimes describe thrust that persists without continuous drive. For RF purposes, expect no intentional radio-frequency (RF) drive. Any SLW/SW or leftover A-potential signature in this mode would be surprising and interesting.

Still structure observation windows around the A/B detector bands (~1.296 GHz, 433.59 MHz) in case of broadband or slowly drifting excess.

Modulated mode

Deliberately modulate the capacitor (or bias) near 1.296 GHz and 433.59 MHz so Dan’s planned bifilar (A) and sphere (B) detectors are on-frequency. This creates a forced opportunity for narrowband detection if any longitudinal/scalar channel couples to the drive.

Modulation also lets you hunt sidebands and harmonics on a wider spectrum analyzer (SA) sweep — without pretending the antennas are calibrated longitudinal sensors yet.

4. Broader radio-frequency (RF) slice

A TinySA Ultra / similar spectrum analyzer covers a much wider band than the two intended transmit (TX) tones. Use the A/B antennas as opportunistic wideband probes while still highlighting planned narrowband locks:

  • Narrowband locks: 1.296 GHz (A), 433.59 MHz (B) — primary hypothesis windows.

  • Broadband plasma / breakdown noise: if vacuum or high-voltage (HV) stress produces arcs or glow — log it; do not romanticize it as SLW.
  • Spurious lab RF: Industrial, Scientific, and Medical (ISM) bands, ham, Wi-Fi, and chamber pumps can masquerade as “signal.”

5. Scalar Wave (SW) / static vector potential / Nikolova–Zimmerman (NZ)–style detectors

Beyond bifilar TEM-null SLW paths, the literature and correspondence tradition discuss detectors more suited to SW or non-propagating vector-potential ideas:

  • Nikolova–Zimmerman-style argon–mercury / neon plasma-tube (fluorescent-tube lineage) detectors as a future method — cite the plasma-tube / fluorescent detection papers carefully; they are not a substitute for a built A/B chain.
  • Contrast with Hively bifilar TEM-null SLW path: designed to favor longitudinal electric coupling while rejecting TEM.
  • Educational Hively correspondence framing (contested): SLW may couple to matter with Ohmic-like σ E∥2 losses; SW (pure dynamical scalar, longitudinal E → 0) is sometimes predicted as comparatively Ohmic-free. Treat this as a hypothesis language, not textbook Maxwell.

6. Math — Hively Extended Electrodynamics (EED) momentum / energy (educational)

Illustrative structure from public Hively / EED educational materials — not a claim that these equations are experimentally confirmed in Titusville.

A useful bookkeeping device in this extended-electrodynamics conversation is the dynamical scalar

C ≡ ∇·A + ∂t φ (with A the vector potential, φ the scalar potential; notation varies across authors — illustrative)

In Lorenz gauge, classical Maxwell effectively sets C = 0. Extended treatments that retain C ≠ 0 discuss additional channels:

  • SLW: propagating modes with a longitudinal electric field component E∥.
  • SW: often framed as a pure-C / scalar channel with little classical E — and, in some correspondence, little or no classical electromagnetic momentum (mark as hypothesis / contested).

Longitudinal drive & gradient current (schematic)

E∥ ~ −∇φ − ∂tA∥ (longitudinal pieces) jgrad ~ σ E∥ (Ohmic-like response to longitudinal E) POhm, SLW ~ σ |E∥|2 (loss channel discussed for SLW) SW (E→0, C≠0): POhm ~ 0 in the “Ohmic-free” sketch — contested

Energy / momentum bookkeeping contrast (qualitative)

Classical TEM (schematic): uEM ~ (ε0/2)|E|2 + (1/(2μ0))|B|2 S ~ E × H (Poynting) SLW sketch: carries longitudinal E → can source Ohmic σE∥2 momentum/energy shared with matter more “electromagnetically familiar” SW sketch (hypothesis): energy associated with C / potentials classical electromagnetic (EM) momentum often argued small or absent → any force bookkeeping must be stated carefully (contested)

Do not invent numerical energy densities or peer-reviewed Hively thrust numbers here. The point for Experiment E is operational: TEM-null antennas plus plasma-tube futures ask whether any excess tracks SLW-like vs SW-like expectations under null vs modulated capacitor modes.

7. Math — Buhler 3rd-order Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) (from public Alternative Propulsion Engineering Conference (APEC) / YouTube)

From public APEC writeups and YouTube talks (e.g. Tim Ventura’s Exodus Propellantless Propulsion Physics and the talk Exodus Propellantless Propulsion Physics | Charles Buhler), the educational structure is:

  • 2nd-order QED perturbation recovers classical Coulomb interaction.
  • 3rd-order involves many more diagrams — public talks cite on the order of ~12 terms (vs fewer at 2nd order; “12 vs 4” appears in talk framing).
  • Charge-asymmetric structures such as Q³ and Q1² Q2 terms appear in the narrative.
  • Feynman-diagram language: virtual photons emitted but not re-absorbed → proposed quantum recoil / momentum available to the charged system.
  • Casimir analogy is used carefully in talks: Exodus narrative emphasizes adding vacuum states, not subtracting them as in Casimir plates.
  • A peer-reviewed formula paper is intended / unpublished as of those public talks — do not invent equations that pretend to be that paper.
Qualitative ladder (public-talk level — not a derived paper): 2nd order → Coulomb-like force between charges 3rd order → additional charge-asymmetric terms (Q³, Q₁²Q₂, …) → diagrams with unpaired virtual-photon lines → proposed net translational force / vacuum momentum exchange No peer-reviewed closed-form thrust formula is reproduced here. Company/APEC claims of multi-lab verification should be read as claims — warm skepticism applies. Buhler emphasizes independence from NASA views.

8. Theory matching — Buhler ↔ Hively (exploratory)

Do not claim equivalence. QED perturbation diagrams and extended Maxwell (EED/MCE) may simply be different languages aimed at different observables. Warm skepticism is the house style.

Clues worth staring at — without forcing a marriage:

  • (b) Buhler’s 3rd-order virtual-photon diagrams that appear to move momentum off the charged system / into vacuum bookkeeping.

11. Pass / fail & controls

  • TEM floors: loop and single-filar antennas — establish residual TEM before celebrating TEM-null excess.
  • Faraday cage: on / off around the capacitor (and document chamber wall effects).
  • Vacuum: on / off — ion wind and Paschen curiosities are classic confounders.
  • Supply: connected vs disconnected (null mode).
  • Modulation: on/off at each band (1.296 GHz, 433.59 MHz).
  • Geometry: distance and orientation sweeps for both chains.
  • Blind-ish logging: pre-register what counts as a “hit” (signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), bandwidth, repeatability) before the visit if possible.

A “pass” for Experiment E would be a reproducible TEM-null excess that tracks capacitor state better than TEM floors and better than lab radio-frequency interference (RFI) — still not automatic proof of SLW/SW physics. A clean “fail” (no excess) is an equally respectable educational outcome.

12. Safety

Exodus lab rules dominate. Dan is a guest with detectors. High-voltage capacitors, vacuum systems, and RF exposure are not casual do-it-yourself (DIY) hazards. Stay outside keep-out zones; follow host personal protective equipment (PPE) and lockout procedures; do not touch chamber HV or override interlocks for a prettier spectrum plot.
  • high-voltage (HV) capacitors store lethal energy even after supply disconnect — verify safe discharge protocols.
  • Vacuum viewports and pumps: eye/ear protection and training as required by the host.
  • RF: keep transmit power (if any guest gear) within agreed limits; respect specific absorption rate (SAR) / exposure common sense near antennas.
  • No unsupervised modulation of host hardware — ask, document, stop when unsure.
