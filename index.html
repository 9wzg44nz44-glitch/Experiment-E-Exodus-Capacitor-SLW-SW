Experiment E — Exodus asymmetric-thrust capacitors vs Hively-style Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) / Scalar Wave (SW) detectors

A short educational sketch of a proposed side-by-side test for Exodus Propulsion Technologies (Titusville, FL; ept.space) and Dr. Lee Hively: whether asymmetric-thrust capacitors emit detectable Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) / Scalar Wave (SW) signatures beside Dan’s planned one Pelican-case equivalent (both the 1.3 GHz Experiment A and 433 MHz Experiment B detectors). Detectors are not built or verified yet. Warm skepticism welcome — published work by others (Hively, Monstein–Wesley) is encouragement, not Dan’s result.

1. Hypothesis

Do Exodus asymmetric electrostatic “thrust capacitors” radiate detectable Scalar Longitudinal Waves (SLW) and/or Scalar Waves (SW) in the sense of Hively’s Maxwell–Cartesian Electrodynamics (MCE) / Extended Electrodynamics (EED) framing, or leave a static / slowly varying vector-potential signature — while classical Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) fields are rejected by TEM-null antenna designs?

The capacitor may sit in a vacuum chamber with a Faraday cage around it, hypothetically transmitting (or not) in both static and modulated modes near 1.3 GHz and 433 MHz. Exodus public materials emphasize electrostatic pressure / third-order Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) narratives for thrust; this Experiment E asks a different, adjacent question: is there a radio-frequency (RF) / potential channel a Hively-style observer might notice?

Skeptical baseline. Null results are scientifically useful. A positive TEM-null excess would still need brutal controls before anyone whispers “longitudinal.” Dan has not yet built Experiment A (≈1.3 GHz bifilar/Faraday) or Experiment B (433 MHz sphere) detectors.

2. Two capacitor modes

Static null mode

Capacitor charged; power supply removed; Exodus-style claims sometimes describe thrust that persists without continuous drive. For RF purposes, expect no intentional radio-frequency (RF) drive. Any SLW/SW or leftover A-potential signature in this mode would be surprising and interesting.

Still structure observation windows around the A/B detector bands (~1.296 GHz, 433.59 MHz) in case of broadband or slowly drifting excess.

Modulated mode

Deliberately modulate the capacitor (or bias) near 1.296 GHz and 433.59 MHz so Dan’s planned bifilar (A) and sphere (B) detectors are on-frequency. This creates a forced opportunity for narrowband detection if any longitudinal/scalar channel couples to the drive.

Modulation also lets you hunt sidebands and harmonics on a wider spectrum analyzer (SA) sweep — without pretending the antennas are calibrated longitudinal sensors yet.

4. Broader radio-frequency (RF) slice

A TinySA Ultra / similar spectrum analyzer covers a much wider band than the two intended transmit (TX) tones. Use the A/B antennas as opportunistic wideband probes while still highlighting planned narrowband locks:

  • Narrowband locks: 1.296 GHz (A), 433.59 MHz (B) — primary hypothesis windows.
  • Harmonics & sidebands: especially under deliberate modulation.
  • Broadband plasma / breakdown noise: if vacuum or high-voltage (HV) stress produces arcs or glow — log it; do not romanticize it as SLW.
  • Spurious lab RF: Industrial, Scientific, and Medical (ISM) bands, ham, Wi-Fi, and chamber pumps can masquerade as “signal.”

5. Things to consider and check — Titusville “listen to the capacitors”

If Dan ever runs a lab RF / field listen next to Exodus hardware in Titusville, treat the items below as a pre-flight checklist — not a claim that a tunneling-launched Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) will be heard. Warm skepticism: napkin math says a post-isolation tunneling → SLW chain is typically far below TinySA Ultra detection at Experiment A/B distances. A null is compatible with “too weak,” not with “thrust is false.”

Lab reality at Exodus (plan around this)

  • Capacitors under test are always in a Faraday cage.
  • At higher voltage they are usually also in a vacuum chamber.
  • Any listen must account for cage attenuation, chamber wall modes, feedthrough / viewport leakage, pump and HV-supply RF hash, and whether the probe is inside the cage, outside the cage, or outside the chamber.

Detectors for this listen (no polarizing rods)

  • Experiment A (~¾″ aluminum sphere + SMA) and/or Experiment B (2.5″ sphere + SMA) as receive probes — without Exp B polarizing rods for this Exodus listen.
  • Spheres alone are crude E-field / RF probes, not an SLW-identifying polarimeter. A TinySA spike ≠ “proven SLW”; at best “RF or pickup coincident with the DUT.”
  • Do not stay parked only on 433 MHz or ~1.3 GHz. Tunneling / dielectric relaxation / charge rearrange are not coherent ham-band beacons. Use wide TinySA Ultra sweeps (span + averaging); treat 433 / 1296 as optional kit check bands.

Tekbox H-probes · B-field null (Hively — required for SLW/SW vs TEM)

Dr. Hively: place a magnetic-field probe near the receiver whenever measuring for SLW or SW (Exp A/B spheres, Exodus listen, etc.). His IARD_2020 talk and earlier work (including 2016 patent-era setups) note prior experiments that failed to measure a B-field in configurations he interpreted as non-TEM — that absence is part of how one argues SLW/SW versus TEM. B-field knowledge is necessary before saying “we detected an SLW or SW rather than a TEM wave.”

  • Candidate on sphere TinySA → immediately scan with H20 (then H10/H5) at the same band next to the RX sphere; try loop orientations.
  • Sphere hit + H at noise → consistent with weak-B / non-TEM hypothesis (still not a proof).
  • Sphere hit + clear H line → TEM or mixed — do not claim pure SLW/SW.
  • Use E5 only as E-field corroboration; it does not replace the H-null.
  • Inside/near Faraday cage or vacuum vessel: log probe position relative to cage wall; cage currents can fake H pickup.

Step-by-step instruments · VNA6000 path check + TinySA Ultra listen

Role split (important): the NanoRFE VNA6000 does not passively capture Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) / Scalar Wave (SW) emissions. It measures S11/S21 to its own stimulus (VNA6000 manual). Use the TinySA Ultra as the spectrum analyzer to listen. Use the VNA to prove the sphere/coax/cage path is sane before you trust a waterfall. DUT stays in the Faraday cage; high voltage usually also in vacuum — never put VNA ports on HV electrodes.

A. VNA6000 — on-device button path (probe / cage path)

  1. Attach SMA connector savers. Power on. Open menu (tap empty screen or ENTER).
  2. STIMULUS → START = 50 kHz (or 100 kHz). STIMULUS → STOP = 3 GHz or 6 GHz for a wide check (re-calibrate after any range change).
  3. Sphere S11 (is the probe alive?):
    1. CAL → RESET then CAL → CALIBRATE.
    2. Put coax on Port 1. At the sphere end of that coax: OPEN → menu OPEN; SHORT → SHORT; LOAD → LOAD. Then DONE → SAVE n.
    3. Connect the sphere. DISPLAY → TRACE → TRACE 0 active; DISPLAY → CHANNEL → CH0 REFLECT; DISPLAY → FORMAT → LOGMAG (and/or SMITH).
    4. Expect a structured S11 (not a dead flat −∞). Save a screenshot / export via PC if using NanoVNA-QT.
  4. Cage / chamber attenuation S21 (how deaf is “outside the cage”?):
    1. Place a small inject antenna or open SMA stub inside the cage (cold, HV off) if staff allow; sphere (or second antenna) outside at the listen position.
    2. Wide SOLT: Port 1 cable to inject, Port 2 cable to sphere — CAL → RESET → CALIBRATE, OPEN/SHORT/LOAD at Port 1 cable end, THRU between cable ends, DONE → SAVE.
    3. DISPLAY → CHANNEL → CH1 THROUGH; FORMAT → LOGMAG.
    4. Record S21(f) with cage closed vs cracked open (if safe). That curve is your “spreading + cage wall” budget in dB.
  5. TDR (cables / feedthroughs): set start ~50 kHz, stop high enough for resolution; DISPLAY → TRANSFORM → TRANSFORM ON; low-pass / step as in the manual; set VELOCITY FACTOR for your coax. Hunt bad connectors before blaming SLW.
  6. Optional PC: NanoVNA-QT / VNA View → Device → Sweep parameters (start/stop, points, averaging) → Calibration pane SOL/SOLT → Apply → export.

B. TinySA Ultra — wideband emission listen (the actual “capture”)

  1. Sphere → coax → TinySA RF in. Start with attenuator auto; keep peaks ≪ 0 dBm (prefer < −25 dBm at the connector).
  2. Enable Ultra mode if listening above ~800 MHz (per TinySA Ultra docs; unlock as required on your unit).
  3. Do not stay only on 433 MHz / 1.3 GHz. Example campaign: (i) 0.1–200 MHz, (ii) 200–800 MHz, (iii) 0.8–3 GHz, (iv) optional 3–6 GHz Ultra. Use narrower RBW + averaging when hunting weak lines; wider RBW for survey speed.
  4. For weakest floors: LNA on, RBW down (e.g. toward 200 Hz–few kHz) — TinySA Ultra can approach roughly −145 dBm discernible at 30 MHz / 200 Hz RBW with LNA (spec sheet order-of-magnitude; tinysa.org Ultra specs). Wide spans raise the effective floor; Ultra mode loses ~10 dB above ~2.5 GHz and more above ~5.3 GHz.
  5. Baselines (same sphere position): HV off; DUT cold; probe capped 50 Ω; if allowed, empty-cage. Then charge / hold / disconnect / isolated-hold.
  6. Save waterfalls + note vacuum on/off, cage closed, distance, and any glow/arc.

C. Where to put the sphere(s) — near field vs far field + 1/r²

Let D be a characteristic size of the cage/DUT (often ~0.1–0.5 m). Wavelength λ = c/f. Rough radiated far-field rule of thumb: r ≳ 2 D² / λ. Reactive near-field scale: r ≲ λ / (2π). In the radiated far field, power density for a fixed radiated power falls ~1/r² (−6 dB each time you double range). Inside/near the cage, decay can be steeper or messy (evanescent / multipath).

  • Near-field / close listen (outside the closed cage wall): start about 5–20 cm from the cage surface (or the nearest allowed point on the vacuum vessel). Goal: maximize weak coupled E before 1/r² kills you. Watch common-mode / ground loops.
  • Transition / mid: about 0.5–1.5 m — compare level vs the close station; if a real radiated line exists, expect roughly order-of −6 to −20 dB depending on exact ranges and pattern.
  • Far-field survey: about 2–3 m (and farther if the room allows), especially below a few hundred MHz where λ/(2π) is large. Use this to test “lab EMI vs DUT.”
  • Two-sphere idea: one close (5–20 cm), one far (2–3 m), same TinySA via switch or two captures. A true free-space-ish radiator should drop ~1/r² between stations; a local pickup / supply hash often will not.
  • Ballpark sensitivity honesty: even at −120 dBm on TinySA, converting to field strength needs antenna factor; a tunneling-level hypothesized SLW after isolation is still likely below this kit at Titusville distances. Use the VNA S21 cage curve + 1/r² to decide whether a claimed line is physically plausible.

Example: if S21 cage path is already −40 dB and you move from 0.15 m to 1.5 m (−20 dB from 1/r² alone), you need ~60 dB more source strength at the DUT to hold the same TinySA reading — plan expectations accordingly.

6. Scalar Longitudinal Wave (SLW) vs Scalar Wave (SW) framing

Experiment E’s planned kit is the A/B Pelican pair (bifilar / sphere). Hively educational correspondence still distinguishes two bookkeeping languages:

  • Hively bifilar TEM-null SLW path: designed to favor longitudinal electric coupling while rejecting TEM.
  • Educational framing (contested): SLW may couple to matter with Ohmic-like σ E∥2 losses; SW (pure dynamical scalar, longitudinal E → 0) is sometimes predicted as comparatively Ohmic-free. Treat this as hypothesis language, not textbook Maxwell.

7. Math — Hively Extended Electrodynamics (EED) momentum / energy (educational)

Illustrative structure from public Hively / EED educational materials — not a claim that these equations are experimentally confirmed in Titusville.

A useful bookkeeping device in this extended-electrodynamics conversation is the dynamical scalar

C ≡ ∇·A + ∂t φ (with A the vector potential, φ the scalar potential; notation varies across authors — illustrative)

In Lorenz gauge, classical Maxwell effectively sets C = 0. Extended treatments that retain C ≠ 0 discuss additional channels:

  • SLW: propagating modes with a longitudinal electric field component E∥.
  • SW: often framed as a pure-C / scalar channel with little classical E — and, in some correspondence, little or no classical electromagnetic momentum (mark as hypothesis / contested).

Longitudinal drive & gradient current (schematic)

E∥ ~ −∇φ − ∂tA∥ (longitudinal pieces) jgrad ~ σ E∥ (Ohmic-like response to longitudinal E) POhm, SLW ~ σ |E∥|2 (loss channel discussed for SLW) SW (E→0, C≠0): POhm ~ 0 in the “Ohmic-free” sketch — contested

Energy / momentum bookkeeping contrast (qualitative)

Classical TEM (schematic): uEM ~ (ε0/2)|E|2 + (1/(2μ0))|B|2 S ~ E × H (Poynting) SLW sketch: carries longitudinal E → can source Ohmic σE∥2 momentum/energy shared with matter more “electromagnetically familiar” SW sketch (hypothesis): energy associated with C / potentials classical electromagnetic (EM) momentum often argued small or absent → any force bookkeeping must be stated carefully (contested)

Do not invent numerical energy densities or peer-reviewed Hively thrust numbers here. The point for Experiment E is operational: TEM-null A/B antennas ask whether any excess tracks SLW-like vs SW-like expectations under null vs modulated capacitor modes.

8. Math — Buhler 3rd-order Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) (from public Alternative Propulsion Engineering Conference (APEC) / YouTube)

From public APEC writeups and YouTube talks (e.g. Tim Ventura’s Exodus Propellantless Propulsion Physics and the talk Exodus Propellantless Propulsion Physics | Charles Buhler), the educational structure is:

  • 2nd-order QED perturbation recovers classical Coulomb interaction.
  • 3rd-order involves many more diagrams — public talks cite on the order of ~12 terms (vs fewer at 2nd order; “12 vs 4” appears in talk framing).
  • Charge-asymmetric structures such as Q³ and Q1² Q2 terms appear in the narrative.
  • Feynman-diagram language: virtual photons emitted but not re-absorbed → proposed quantum recoil / momentum available to the charged system.
  • Casimir analogy is used carefully in talks: Exodus narrative emphasizes adding vacuum states, not subtracting them as in Casimir plates.
  • A peer-reviewed formula paper is intended / unpublished as of those public talks — do not invent equations that pretend to be that paper.
Qualitative ladder (public-talk level — not a derived paper): 2nd order → Coulomb-like force between charges 3rd order → additional charge-asymmetric terms (Q³, Q₁²Q₂, …) → diagrams with unpaired virtual-photon lines → proposed net translational force / vacuum momentum exchange No peer-reviewed closed-form thrust formula is reproduced here. Company/APEC claims of multi-lab verification should be read as claims — warm skepticism applies. Buhler emphasizes independence from NASA views.

Patent vs talk gap

The patent path (WO2020159603 family and US grants/applications listed on ept.space/patents) is largely classical asymmetrical electrostatic pressure language. Public talks add a separate 3rd-order QED narrative. Experiment E notes the gap; it does not paper over it.

9. Theory matching — Buhler ↔ Hively (exploratory)

Do not claim equivalence. QED perturbation diagrams and extended Maxwell (EED/MCE) may simply be different languages aimed at different observables. Warm skepticism is the house style.

Clues worth staring at — without forcing a marriage:

  • (a) EED’s separate SLW vs SW energy/momentum terms — SW often sketched with little classical momentum, like a “pure scalar” channel.
  • (b) Buhler’s 3rd-order virtual-photon diagrams that appear to move momentum off the charged system / into vacuum bookkeeping.

  4. Under null vs modulated modes, does any TEM-null excess look more “SLW-like” or “SW-like” in the contested bookkeeping — or simply like residual TEM / lab RFI?

12. Pass / fail & controls

  • TEM floors: loop and single-filar antennas — establish residual TEM before celebrating TEM-null excess.
  • Faraday cage: on / off around the capacitor (and document chamber wall effects).
  • Vacuum: on / off — ion wind and Paschen curiosities are classic confounders.
  • Supply: connected vs disconnected (null mode).
  • Modulation: on/off at each band (1.296 GHz, 433.59 MHz).
  • Geometry: distance and orientation sweeps for both chains.
  • Blind-ish logging: pre-register what counts as a “hit” (signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), bandwidth, repeatability) before the visit if possible.

A “pass” for Experiment E would be a reproducible TEM-null excess that tracks capacitor state better than TEM floors and better than lab radio-frequency interference (RFI) — still not automatic proof of SLW/SW physics. A clean “fail” (no excess) is an equally respectable educational outcome.

13. Safety

Exodus lab rules dominate. Dan is a guest with detectors. High-voltage capacitors, vacuum systems, and RF exposure are not casual do-it-yourself (DIY) hazards. Stay outside keep-out zones; follow host personal protective equipment (PPE) and lockout procedures; do not touch chamber HV or override interlocks for a prettier spectrum plot.
  • high-voltage (HV) capacitors store lethal energy even after supply disconnect — verify safe discharge protocols.
  • Vacuum viewports and pumps: eye/ear protection and training as required by the host.
  • RF: keep transmit power (if any guest gear) within agreed limits; respect specific absorption rate (SAR) / exposure common sense near antennas.
  • No unsupervised modulation of host hardware — ask, document, stop when unsure.
